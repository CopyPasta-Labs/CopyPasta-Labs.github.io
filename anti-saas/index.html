AntiSaaS Manifesto

Traditional SaaS gives every customer the same rented tool. AntiSaaS gives each customer an owned tool made for them.

The traditional SaaS problem

  • A SaaS company builds one standard application for many customers.
  • Each customer must adapt their work to the application’s features, limits, and product roadmap.
  • The customer must keep paying to use the same tool and access their own workflow.
  • AI can now make bespoke software around one customer’s exact goals and methods.

The AntiSaaS opportunity

  • A SaaS company’s strongest asset is its knowledge of the customer’s problem.
  • Its current application is only one expression of that knowledge.
  • The company can become a personal software factory instead of renting one fixed product.
  • Each customer gets an AI domain expert and an AI software engineer.
  • The domain expert learns the customer’s goals, language, methods, and problems.
  • The engineer builds a personal system around that model.
  • The result can be a new application or a control layer over the customer’s existing tools.

The customer owns the result

  • The customer owns the application, source code, data, rules, and configuration.
  • The software continues to work without a subscription.
  • The customer can modify, host, or sell the software.
  • The customer can replace the SaaS company, AI model, or maintenance provider.
  • Maintenance remains optional, competitive, and replaceable.

The software improves with the customer

  • Each application starts with one measurable goal.
  • Usage, feedback, and metrics show where the application fails.
  • AI agents propose and build changes.
  • Tests prove that a change did not break the application.
  • Metrics prove whether the change produced a better result.
  • The loop is simple: measure, improve, regenerate.

The control layer belongs to the customer

  • A local control layer stores the customer’s rules, tool mappings, memory, and safety limits.
  • It can coordinate existing tools instead of forcing all data into a new silo.
  • Both people and AI agents can read and change it.
  • The customer’s workflow survives when an application or AI provider changes.

The business still makes money

  • The company can charge for the first build.
  • It can charge for expert sessions, major changes, support, or optional hosting.
  • It sells expertise and verified improvement instead of access to one fixed application.
  • The customer never pays rent only to keep access to their own workflow.

The transition for a traditional SaaS company

  • Your current application is not your strongest asset.
  • Your strongest asset is your knowledge of the customer’s problem.
  • Turn that knowledge into bespoke software for each customer.
  • Give the customer ownership.
  • Improve the software against measured results.
  • Sell creation, expertise, and optional service instead of permanent access.

The AntiSaaS formula

  • A domain expert.
  • An AI software engineer.
  • User-owned software and data.
  • A user-owned control layer.
  • One measurable goal.
  • A tested regeneration loop.
  • Optional and replaceable maintenance.

Do not rent the application. Own the software, the data, and the improvement loop.
